# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Musicians, bands, and independent artists based in or near Quezon City, Philippines. They come to Seven Lions to rehearse with a full backline, record demos or full productions, learn guitar or drums, or get an instrument serviced. They are price-conscious and practical: they read the inclusions list, they check the pricing, and they book when they're confident. A second user group is studio staff and the single admin managing bookings and service requests through the admin panel.

Scene: a guitarist scrolling the site on a phone after band practice, mid-evening, deciding whether to book a slot for next weekend. Low patience for fluff; high attention to specifics.

## Product Purpose

A studio management platform for Seven Lions Studio. Online presence plus a booking and service-request system. Musicians discover services, see availability, and submit booking or service requests. The admin approves or rejects them, manages the roster of studio artists (bands), and monitors activity through a calendar and request log.

Success: a musician lands on the site, understands what's offered and what it costs, and submits a booking in under three minutes without confusion.

## Brand Personality

Precision, Craft, Underground. Not a lifestyle brand. Not a community hub. A serious space for serious work, run by people who know what they're doing. Accessible to beginners but designed for musicians who care about the result.

## Anti-references

- **Spotify / streaming apps**: consumer music product vibes, lush photography grids, neon green, playlists, discovery feeds. This is a venue, not a platform.
- **SoundCloud / Bandcamp**: indie music platform aesthetics, waveform UIs, orange-heavy, "upload your music here" energy.
- **Hotel and event venue sites**: serif font excess, white space as luxury, lifestyle photography of laughing people, soft ambient photography, aspirational copy that says nothing.

## Design Principles

1. **Clarity is the craft.** The interface should be as precise and well-set-up as the studio equipment. Every element earns its position. Ambiguity is a malfunction.
2. **Dark by necessity.** Musicians work in low-light environments. The dark interface is not a trend choice; it follows the user's context. It should feel like the room, not like a website imitating a room.
3. **Utility earns the aesthetic.** Beautiful only where it serves the experience. Decoration that doesn't carry information gets cut.
4. **Local, not aspirational.** This is a specific place for specific people. Copy and design should reflect that: direct, unpretentious, no lifestyle photography clichés.
5. **Trust through specificity.** Show the details. Pricing, inclusions, policies, turnaround times. Musicians read carefully before they commit money and time. Reward that attention.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA minimum. `prefers-reduced-motion` respected on all animations (already implemented). Text contrast maintained against dark backgrounds. Forms fully keyboard-navigable.
